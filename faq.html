Section B — How the Optimizer Works

ChoreMatrix calculates plans using a deterministic constraint-satisfaction and scoring engine. When you request an optimization:

  • The engine normalizes all task occurrences across your requested planning horizon (1 to 4 weeks).
  • It filters potential candidate assignments against strict hard constraints (availability, eligibility, and fixed days).
  • It evaluates eligible assignments using weighted criteria—penalizing workload imbalance, daily workload spikes, and avoided tasks, while rewarding preferred tasks and balanced cadence.
  • It refines the schedule through systematic local search improvements (2-opt candidate swaps).

Because the engine is deterministic, identical inputs and constraints always produce the exact same verifiable allocation without random drift.

Equal Workload (balanced mode) is an optimization objective designed for households where participants intend to split domestic chores evenly—such as roommates or adult partners with similar schedules.

In this mode, the optimizer minimizes the difference in total weighted chore minutes assigned to each person, striving to distribute total chore volume equally across all participants.

Capacity-Aware optimization distributes work proportionally according to each member's declared weekly capacity minutes.

This mode is essential for families with teenagers, children, or partners working disparate outside hours. For example, if Member A has 300 minutes of weekly chore capacity and Member B has 150 minutes, Capacity-Aware optimization assigns Member A approximately double the weighted chore load of Member B, targeting an equal percentage of capacity utilization rather than an identical number of minutes.

Effort reflects the physical and mental intensity required to complete a task. ChoreMatrix uses an explicit 5-point effort multiplier scale:

  • Level 1 (1.00x multiplier): Light, low-friction activities (e.g. wiping down a kitchen counter).
  • Level 2 (1.25x multiplier): Routine maintenance chores (e.g. emptying the dishwasher).
  • Level 3 (1.50x multiplier): Standard active chores (e.g. vacuuming the living room, routine cooking).
  • Level 4 (1.85x multiplier): Demanding physical labor (e.g. scrubbing bathrooms, thorough mopping).
  • Level 5 (2.25x multiplier): Heavy or exhausting chores (e.g. deep oven cleaning, extensive yard work).

Raw task duration is multiplied by this factor to calculate "weighted minutes," ensuring 30 minutes of deep scrubbing is accurately weighted higher than 30 minutes of folding light laundry.

Hard constraints are mandatory scheduling boundaries that the optimizer will never violate. Common examples include:

  • Unavailable days: If a member is traveling or working late on Thursdays, the optimizer cannot assign chores to them on Thursdays.
  • Eligibility restrictions: If a chore is flagged only for adults (e.g. using chemical detergents or power tools), younger members are strictly excluded.
  • Fixed-day tasks: Chores that must occur on a specific day of the week (such as putting out trash cans on Sunday evening) will only ever be placed on that designated day.

If a hard constraint cannot be satisfied, the chore is flagged as unassigned rather than assigned improperly.

Soft preferences represent non-binding inclinations, such as a member's preferred tasks, disliked tasks, or preferred execution days.

The optimizer factors preferences into candidate scoring by applying bonuses when preferred tasks are assigned and applying penalties when assigning avoided chores. Preferences guide the engine toward agreeable arrangements, but they will yield when necessary to satisfy hard constraints or maintain workload feasibility.

Section C — Calculate vs Rebalance

Calculate Plan generates an optimized schedule from scratch based on your current household members, capacities, and chore lists.

It considers the entire planning horizon without being anchored to past allocations, establishing the most balanced baseline schedule possible. It is best used when setting up your household for the first time or creating a brand-new weekly cycle.

Rebalance performs minimal-disruption rescheduling when circumstances change mid-week or mid-cycle.

Instead of throwing out your settled schedule and reassigning everything, the Rebalance engine applies a specific penalty to moving tasks that are already scheduled. It keeps unaffected tasks in place and calculates the smallest necessary adjustments to accommodate the new conditions, accompanied by an itemized diff summary explaining what changed and why.

Use Rebalance whenever unexpected schedule adjustments occur in an active plan.

Concrete Example: Suppose a household member falls ill or gets called away on Wednesday. You edit that member's availability to mark Wednesday as an unavailable day, then click Rebalance. Rather than rewriting Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday chores for everyone, ChoreMatrix preserves unaffected tasks and moves only that member's Wednesday responsibilities to available, eligible members.

Section D — Results & Diagnostics

The fairness index (or balance score) is a mathematical metric reflecting the spread in capacity utilization across household members. A score of 100 indicates that all members have an identical workload utilization relative to their capacity.

This metric does not claim to represent objective moral justice or philosophical fairness for your specific family. It simply measures how evenly the workload is distributed according to the numbers, capacities, and constraints you provided. If tight constraints force one person to carry a higher utilization, the balance score decreases accordingly.

When a member's utilization exceeds 100%, it means they have been assigned more weighted chore minutes than their declared weekly availability. ChoreMatrix surfaces this honestly as an overload diagnostic.

Overload typically occurs when household chore demands exceed total available member hours, or when strict eligibility rules leave only one specific member qualified to perform demanding tasks. ChoreMatrix flags the capacity deficit rather than masking the imbalance.

If a task cannot be assigned without breaking a hard constraint, ChoreMatrix lists it in an explicit Unassigned Tasks diagnostic table.

Each unassigned entry includes an explicit diagnostic code (such as ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEMBERS or ERR_ALL_ELIGIBLE_UNAVAILABLE) and specific remediation suggestions. ChoreMatrix never silently invents invalid assignments or forces a chore onto an ineligible person.

Infeasibility indicates that the combination of requested constraints mathematically leaves zero valid allocation options.

For instance, if a chore is locked to Friday, requires an adult, and all adult members are marked as unavailable on Fridays, no schedule can be generated that satisfies every rule. Infeasibility diagnostics highlight these conflicting constraints so you can adjust task days, expand eligibility, or update availability.

  • Sign Out Everywhere: Invalidates refresh tokens across all devices simultaneously.
