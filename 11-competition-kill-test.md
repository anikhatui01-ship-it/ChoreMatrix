# 11 — Competition Kill Test

## Question

Is ChoreMatrix sufficiently differentiated from current household chore and fairness products to justify a new web SaaS?

## Direct competition found

### FairChore

Household chore allocation, effort-aware workload balancing, household sharing and premium features.

Source: https://www.fairchore.com/en/best-chore-apps

### Equal House

Fairness engine, effort weighting, automatic balancing, schedules and household workload concepts.

Source: https://equal.house/features/fairness-engine/

### Duety

Effort weighting, household tasks, fairness analytics, schedules and vacation/availability features.

Source: https://duety.app/

### Tody

Large cleaning/household product with automatic rotation and household sharing.

Source: https://todyapp.com/

### Cozi

Large family organizer with chores among broader family-management functionality.

Source: https://www.cozi.com/

## What is already commoditized

- task lists
- recurring chores
- reminders
- points
- completion history
- household sharing
- basic fairness percentages
- rotation

## Surviving wedge

ChoreMatrix should lead with:

> **Describe the household once. Calculate the workable allocation.**

The product should optimize a household across:

- number of people
- available time
- task frequency
- task duration
- eligibility
- exclusions
- day constraints
- household type

## Main business risks

### Risk 1 — One-time value

A household may only need the optimizer once.

**Counter:** make replanning and weekly/rotational use genuinely useful before adding tracking.

### Risk 2 — Setup friction

Users may abandon the form before the first result.

**Counter:** presets + starter task bundles + progressive disclosure.

### Risk 3 — Perceived unfairness

The algorithm may be mathematically balanced but socially unacceptable.

**Counter:** explainability + user-adjustable weights + explicit constraints.

### Risk 4 — Competition copies the idea

The algorithm is reproducible.

**Counter:** better UX, wider household coverage, data from accepted/rejected plans, better constraint handling and strong SEO distribution.

### Risk 5 — Google search is crowded

"Chore chart" is a large term, but many results are printable charts, established apps and content sites.

**Counter:** target high-intent long-tail tools such as household chore planner, chore schedule generator, family chore chart generator and workload calculator while building a distinctive brand.

## Kill criteria

Kill or reposition the product if user testing shows any of these:

- <30% of qualified visitors reach the first plan
- median setup exceeds 5 minutes despite presets
- <20% of testers say the generated plan is usable without major editing
- most users say a static chart is sufficient
- repeated users do not return for recalculation/replanning
- no clear paid feature is discovered after usability testing
