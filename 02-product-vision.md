# 02 — Product Vision

## Vision

Make household responsibility allocation computational instead of repeatedly negotiated from scratch.

## Product thesis

The household has a limited resource: people-hours.

The household also has recurring work, constraints and preferences.

ChoreMatrix treats the problem as a constrained allocation problem rather than as a checklist problem.

## What we want the user to feel

Not:

> "Great, another app I have to maintain."

But:

> "I gave it the messy details and it figured out the plan."

## Product moat hypothesis

The long-term moat should not be the task list. Task lists are commodities.

The stronger moat is:

- household-aware optimization
- constraint handling
- explanation quality
- fast replanning when reality changes
- broad household configuration support
- learned preferences from accepted/rejected assignments, if later validated

## Product principles

### Solve before asking for an account

Do not gate the first useful result behind signup.

### Constraints over decoration

A useful optimizer needs to understand capacity, eligibility and availability before it needs themes, avatars or badges.

### Explain the result

Users need to understand why work landed where it did.

### Detect impossible plans

When total work exceeds available capacity, the application should surface a shortage rather than fake fairness.

### Minimize setup effort

Pre-populated tasks and household presets should reduce manual input.

### Don't pretend fairness is objective

The product can optimize the selected model; it cannot determine an absolute moral definition of fair.

## Future vision

Possible future directions after product-market evidence:

- automatic weekly rollover
- quick household change handling
- task swaps
- household history
- personalized optimization preferences
- shared live household view
- optional reminders
- optional calendar export
