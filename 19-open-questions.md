# 19 — Open Questions & Experiments

## Highest-priority questions

### 1. Is the optimizer valuable enough without tracking?

Experiment: give users a generated plan and ask them to use it for one week.

### 2. Do people prefer equal workload or capacity-aware workload?

Experiment: show the same household in both modes and observe which result they choose.

### 3. How much setup is tolerable?

Experiment: test preset bundles vs fully custom task creation.

### 4. Do users trust effort weights?

Experiment: allow simple duration only in one flow and duration + effort in another.

### 5. Is the strongest user job planning, fairness, or reducing mental load?

Interview/behavioral experiment. Do not decide from survey adjectives alone.

### 6. Will users return?

Test re-entry around real schedule changes rather than generic reminders.

### 7. What is the right paid value?

Test multiple premium concepts before implementing a large subscription surface.

## Potential strategic pivots

If the product is repeatedly used only once:

- consider a one-time household planning product
- expand into periodic replanning
- add shareable household plans

If users love recalculation but not tracking:

- keep the product as an optimizer/calculator
- monetize advanced scenarios

If users want tracking after the result:

- add lightweight tracking without replacing the optimizer core

## Kill criteria

The project should be reconsidered if:

- users cannot reach a first result quickly
- users routinely ignore the generated allocations
- household-specific constraints are too complicated to model cleanly
- most users only want a printable template
- no repeated use case emerges
