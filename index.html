Core Differentiation

Why ChoreMatrix is Different from a Basic Chore Chart

Generic checklist apps only record who checked off a box. ChoreMatrix actively solves the workload allocation puzzle using three deterministic capabilities:

1

Quantified Fairness Index

What it measures: A 0–100 mathematical score representing how evenly workload utilization is spread across household members relative to their capacity.

Scope: It measures proportional time and effort balance — not subjective emotional satisfaction. A score near 100 indicates balanced distribution; lower scores highlight bottleneck constraints or capacity deficits.

2

Two Workload Modes

Capacity-Aware (Proportional): Chores scale to each person's available weekly hours (e.g., 300 min vs 120 min) so busier members carry less work.

Equal Workload (Flatten Minutes): Divides task minutes evenly among all members regardless of schedule differences — ideal for flatmates sharing equal rent and duties.

3

Explainable Assignments

Transparent rationale: Every task in the schedule displays why it was slotted — such as only eligible member, preferred task match, proportional capacity fit, or preferred day alignment.

No black boxes: Understanding why someone received a task prevents household conflict and clarifies trade-offs immediately.
