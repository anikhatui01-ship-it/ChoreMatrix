# 03 — Personas & Household Contexts

## Persona A — Couple with asymmetric schedules

Two adults work different hours. One person currently notices and coordinates most of the household work.

**Needs:** capacity-aware split, recurring chores, easy rebalancing.

**Risk:** a simple 50/50 split may feel fair mathematically but unrealistic in practice.

## Persona B — Family with children/teens

Adults carry higher-capability tasks while younger members can contribute to age-appropriate work.

**Needs:** eligibility, capacity, recurring rotations, visibility.

**Risk:** assigning unsafe or unrealistic tasks undermines trust.

## Persona C — Roommates

Adults have relatively independent schedules and want an explicit division of shared-area work.

**Needs:** shared chores, availability, rotation, balanced workload.

**Risk:** roommate systems often need simple rules, not a complex management platform.

## Persona D — Multigenerational household

Members can have different physical capacities, schedules and preferred tasks.

**Needs:** explicit constraints and capacity-based allocation.

**Risk:** pure equal-count balancing can be obviously wrong.

## Persona E — Custom/shared household

A household with unusual responsibilities, animals, outdoor work or non-standard arrangements.

**Needs:** custom tasks, custom constraints and arbitrary household size.

## Persona priority

MVP should optimize for:

1. Couple
2. Family
3. Roommates

Then validate whether multigenerational/custom cases materially improve retention before adding specialized UX.
